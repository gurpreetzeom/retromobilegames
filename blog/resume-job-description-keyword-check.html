How to compare your resume with a job description

Applying to a role is easier when your resume clearly describes relevant, truthful experience. A keyword comparison is a useful editing aid, but it is not an ATS score or a prediction of getting hired.

1. Read the role for evidence, not just buzzwords

Separate must-have requirements from nice-to-have skills. Note tools, domain experience, responsibilities and outcomes. For example, a product manager role might mention roadmaps, stakeholder alignment, experimentation and SQL.

2. Check what your resume actually demonstrates

Find each requirement in your existing experience. If you have used SQL to measure funnel conversion, write that clearly instead of adding “SQL” to an unsupported skills list. Never claim experience you do not have.

3. Make achievements specific

Compare “Worked on onboarding” with “Redesigned onboarding steps and measured the effect on completion rate.” Where you have verified numbers, include them; where you do not, explain the scope and your contribution without inventing metrics.

4. Check gaps without keyword stuffing

A simple keyword tool can surface words present in the job description but missing from your resume. Review each suggestion manually. Related phrases, context and the quality of evidence matter more than repeating a term.

5. Keep formatting readable

Use clear headings, consistent dates, accessible text and a concise structure. Review the PDF or document before sending it. Employers and applicant systems vary, so no formatting choice guarantees acceptance.
